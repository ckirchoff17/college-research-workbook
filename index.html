College Research Workbook

Figure out what you want in a college, research the schools on your list, and compare them side by side. Use it in any grade and come back to it as your answers change.

How to use this workbook

The workbook has three parts that build on each other. What you decide matters to you in Part 1 shapes how you judge each college in Part 2, and Part 3 pulls it all together into one chart.

  1. About me. Reflect on what you want from college and rate how much each factor matters to you.
  2. My colleges. Add each school you're considering and look up its fit, cost, and admissions details.
  3. Compare. See your schools side by side. You can turn on a fit score based on your own ratings.

Where to focus in your grade

9th grade

Spend most of your time in Part 1. Try researching one or two schools you're curious about just to see what's out there.

10th grade

Revisit Part 1, since your answers will change. Research three to five schools of different sizes and settings, including one close enough to visit.

11th grade

Build your list in Part 2 with a mix of reach, target, and likely schools. Run each school's net price calculator with a parent or guardian.

12th grade

Confirm deadlines and application plans. After decisions come in, add your actual aid offers and use the comparison chart to choose.

Your answers stay on this device. The workbook saves automatically in this browser, and nothing is sent anywhere. If you switch computers or clear your browser, your work won't come with you, so use Download backup now and then and Restore backup to pick up where you left off.

Part 1: About me

There are no right answers here. Be honest about what you want, not what you think colleges want to hear.

Reflect

What matters most

Rate how important each factor is to you, from 1 (not important) to 5 (essential). These ratings power the optional fit score in Part 3.

Part 2: My colleges

Add a page for each school you're considering. You don't have to fill in everything at once; leave blanks and come back as you learn more.

Part 3: Compare

Everything here comes from Part 2. To change something, update that college's page.

Your fit score combines how much each factor matters to you (Part 1) with how well you think each school matches (Part 2), on a scale of 0 to 100. It reflects only your own ratings, so use it to start a conversation with your counselor or family, not to make the decision for you.
